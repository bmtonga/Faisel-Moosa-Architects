Designing Homes for Lusaka's Climate

Lusaka sits on a plateau with strong sun for most of the year, cool dry winters and a short, intense rainy season. Good design here starts long before materials and finishes are chosen: it starts with where the building sits on its plot and which way its rooms face.

Our first move on any residential project is to orient the main living spaces so they receive soft morning light and are shielded from the hot afternoon sun on the west. Bedrooms are placed where they can be cross-ventilated at night, and service rooms take the harshest exposure.

Shade is a design element, not an afterthought

Deep roof overhangs, verandas, screens and planted courtyards all do the same job: they keep direct sun off glass and walls so the building stays cool without relying on air conditioning. We size overhangs for the sun angles of each facade, so winter light still reaches inside while high summer sun is cut off.

The rains need the same attention. Generous gutters, well-drained paving and raised floor levels keep water moving away from the house, and covered outdoor spaces mean the garden is still usable when a storm passes through.

Light without heat

Large openings are welcome when they face the right way. We pair north- and south-facing glazing with narrow, high windows on the east and west, and use light-coloured roofs and walls to reflect heat. The result is a home that is bright all day and noticeably cooler by evening.

If you are planning to build, bring us your plot early. The orientation decisions made in the first sketches are the ones that shape comfort and running costs for the life of the house.
